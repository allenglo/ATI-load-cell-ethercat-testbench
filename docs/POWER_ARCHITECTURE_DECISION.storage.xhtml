Decision

Use a modular three-node power architecture rather than trying to make one IC provide the PSU input, battery, protected load bus, USB-C ports, BMS, fuel gauge, and programmable lab output.

The preferred baseline is BQ25756 + USB-C PD 3.1 EPR controller + smart BMS/fuel gauge + separate digitally controlled output stage.

Required system

  • 24 V PSU input.
  • Configurable 24 V-class battery pack.
  • Protected load bus that remains powered during input loss.
  • Battery charge and battery-to-load UPS operation.
  • Battery percentage, remaining capacity, health and fault reporting.
  • Per-cell monitoring, balancing and hard battery protection.
  • USB-C PD input/output at 140 W or higher, with standard EPR support.
  • At least 10 A capability on the internal/output bus; USB-C remains limited by its negotiated contract.
  • MCU/I2C/SMBus control of voltage, current limits and telemetry.
  • Medical-robotics isolation and fault containment.

Architecture

24 V isolated PSU
    |
 fuse, TVS, reverse protection, hot-swap / ideal diode
    |
    +---- protected load bus
    |
    +---- BQ25756 bidirectional battery stage ---- battery pack
                                      |
                              smart BMS + fuel gauge

 load bus ---- programmable digital DC/DC output ---- protected 0-24 V lab output
 load bus ---- USB PD 3.1 EPR source controller ---- USB-C output

The PSU-to-load path is external to the charger IC. It must provide reverse-current blocking and controlled source/battery arbitration.

Infineon EZ-PD PMG1-B2 + smart BMSPromising future optionHighest integration and firmware control, but newer, more firmware-intensive, and requires validation of high-power source operation and reference design maturity.
ISL81601 / ISL81801 / LT8708Rejected for baselineStrong power stages, but analog control and external ADC/DAC/control-loop work violate the digital-control/minimal-engineering requirement.
BQ25792/BQ25798 and laptop-style chargersRejectedWrong cell count and voltage class for the intended 24-30 V battery system.

Important corrections

  • None of the charger or PD controller candidates is a complete per-cell BMS and accurate coulomb-counting fuel gauge.
  • RAA489118 and RAA489400 are not a complete BMS/SOC solution.
  • PMG1-B2 has MCU and ADC resources but is not a turnkey independent safety BMS or calibrated fuel gauge.
  • ISL81601 is a two-port converter, not a three-node UPS manager.
  • BQ25756 is a smart two-port battery charger/converter. The protected PSU-to-load path still requires external ORing/eFuse/hot-swap design.
  • USB-C 140 W means a standard 28 V at 5 A EPR contract. A 10 A internal bus is a separate power-stage requirement.
  • Dell 130 W and other unofficial fast-charge behavior is a compatibility target, not a guaranteed result of standard USB PD.

Open engineering work

  1. Choose the battery cell count and maximum charge voltage. A 7S LiFePO4 pack provides more margin for a 30 V-class charger; retain BQ25756 for a full 8S/29.2 V pack.
  2. Select a traceable smart BMS and fuel gauge with the required interface and isolation strategy.
  3. Select the USB-C PD EPR source/sink controller and validate the intended 140 W contracts with certified 5 A EPR cables.
  4. Select and validate the separate 0-24 V, 10 A-class programmable output stage.
  5. Define the three-node power-path state machine: PSU present, battery charging, battery backup, source return, low SOC, fault and load shedding.
  6. Complete the medical isolation, leakage-current and fault-containment review with the system safety owner.
  7. Prototype and thermally validate the external-FET, inductor, shunt, connector and eFuse power stages.
