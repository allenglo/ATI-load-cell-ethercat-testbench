Decision

Core: BQ25756 synchronous bidirectional buck-boost charger plus BQ76952 cell-monitor/protection BMS. Add a distinct SOC estimator or fuel gauge, a protected three-node power path, and a separate programmable output stage. Treat USB-C PD as an optional interface add-on.

The main design is not “a charger with a battery attached.” It is a coordinated system with three electrical nodes: 24 V PSU input, battery pack, and protected load bus. BQ25756 is the two-port battery converter inside that system; it does not provide the external PSU-to-load ORing path by itself.

The external input path and the BQ25756 path meet at the protected load bus through designed reverse-current blocking/ORing. The BQ25756 provides controlled energy transfer between its two ports; the system design provides the third node, source arbitration, hold-up and load distribution.

The input may be below the required battery charge voltage; that is why the core stage must be buck-boost, not buck-only. A fully charged 8S LiFePO4 pack is approximately 29.2 V; nominal is approximately 25.6 V. 24 V input cannot directly charge that pack to its target without conversion.

Voltage, current and power limits

  • TI lists BQ25756 as a 4.2–70 V I2C bidirectional buck-boost controller, supporting up to 16S LiFePO4 and up to 20 A charge-current capability under specified external power-stage conditions.
  • The BQ25756EVM is a separate implementation limit: its published configuration is up to 55 V and 10 A charge current. Do not treat IC limits as the EVM or custom-board continuous rating.
  • Every current claim must identify which path it refers to: PSU input current, battery charge/discharge current, load-bus current, or USB-C port current.
  • 24 V at 10 A is 240 W. A 140 W USB-C source cannot continuously supply a 240 W load after conversion losses; the battery can buffer peaks, but the system must budget and limit power.
  • Set charge voltage from cell count, cell data sheet and BMS limits. Do not assume the example 27–28 V standby setting is approved for the selected pack.

What SOC means here

BQ25756 voltage/current telemetry and BQ76952 cell data are measurements, not automatically a reliable battery percentage. For LiFePO4, pack voltage alone is a weak SOC estimate over much of the discharge curve.

A useful SOC path needs signed pack-current measurement, integration during charge and discharge, a stored usable-capacity value, temperature handling, known-full/empty correction and reset-safe persistence. A dedicated fuel gauge reduces the amount of estimator behavior that the system MCU must own. Neither gauge nor charger replaces the BQ76952 cell protections.

Separate 0–24 V, 10 A output

A lab output is a separate power channel, not just BQ25756 reverse mode. The current controller candidate is TI LM51772: a digitally controlled four-switch buck-boost controller with I2C and external FETs. Its published EVM example is 20 V at up to 5 A, not proof of 24 V/10 A continuous operation. Its minimum regulated output is 3.3 V, so zero output requires a separate disable/disconnect.

TPS55288 is not suitable for a 24 V setpoint because its specified maximum output is 21.26 V. Any 240 W output design needs verified continuous thermal capacity, hardware current limit, short-circuit behavior, output eFuse/disconnect, reverse-current protection and suitably rated connectors/copper. A digital setpoint is not a substitute for analog hardware current sensing or independent protection within the converter.

Medical isolation and fault containment

None of the candidate charger, BMS or PD ICs provides medical isolation by itself. Isolation classification, leakage-current limits, creepage/clearance and barriers depend on intended use, applied part, patient connections and system risk analysis; the safety/compliance owner must set these requirements.

  • Start from an appropriately certified isolated 24 V PSU.
  • Keep the BQ76952/BMS local to the pack. Use isolated communications when signals cross a defined isolation boundary; isolated CAN is one possible system interface.
  • Review USB-C shield/VBUS, Ethernet shield, debug connectors, external output returns, chassis and heatsinks as possible isolation paths.
  • Use pack fuse, cell-level BMS cutoff, reverse blocking, surge protection, eFuse, thermal monitoring and safe-disabled outputs independently of supervisory firmware.

Efficiency comparison: BQ25756 vs ISL81601

No defensible winner can be chosen from controller topology alone. Both drive external four-switch power stages; FETs, inductor, shunts, frequency, dead time, layout, gate drive and thermal conditions determine measured efficiency. BQ25756 emphasizes integrated charger policy/I2C and telemetry. ISL81601 offers flexible bidirectional control loops but requires substantially more external analog supervision; this makes it a poorer fit to the digital-control/minimal-engineering requirement, not necessarily a less efficient converter.

For a fair comparison, measure both directions at matched conditions: 24 V source to selected battery charge voltage, then battery to a regulated 24 V bus. Capture 25/50/75/100% load after thermal equilibrium using calibrated simultaneous input/output voltage and current. Record FETs, inductor, shunts, switching frequency, board temperature and airflow. Report eta = Pout / Pin * 100% at each point. Do not compare unrelated datasheet peaks or EVMs at different voltage/current conditions.

MCU and optional USB-C roles

BQ25756 baseline: the system MCU uses I2C to set charge voltage/current, reverse bus target/current limit, read telemetry/faults and execute the PSU/battery state machine. Hardware design must validate the exact command sequencing, reset/watchdog behavior, input-present detection, reverse-mode transitions and recovery against the BQ25756 data sheet and EVM.

PMG1-B2 alternative: combines USB-C PD and a 55 V buck-boost charger controller with an on-chip MCU. It can reduce controller count, but moves PD/charger firmware, integration and validation into that platform. Manufacturer material describes a 240 W sink reference; the listed reference configuration sources 27 W, so high-power source operation must be demonstrated before selecting it for that role.

USB-C is optional: if added, use a dedicated EPR-capable port controller such as TPS26750 for a protected 28 V/5 A (140 W) contract. Include cable validation, VBUS protection/discharge, eFuse and power-budget policy. USB-C is a port feature, not the 10 A internal distribution bus. Dell 130 W behavior is a separate compatibility test, not guaranteed standard PD.

Decision and next engineering gates

Keep BQ25756 as the synchronous buck-boost battery stage and BQ76952 as the per-cell BMS monitor/protector. Add a defined SOC path rather than calling BQ76952 a finished fuel gauge: either implement calibrated SOC in the host using BQ76952 current/coulomb data, or pair it with BQ34Z100-R2 or a traceable smart BMS/gauge module. USB-C remains an optional module.

  1. Freeze pack series count, chemistry, capacity, voltage ceiling, current and temperature limits.
  2. Choose BQ34Z100-R2 or host SOC estimation and validate its pack-current measurement, calibration, persistence and SOC error.
  3. Prototype BQ76952 cell measurement/balancing/protection and prove independent pack disconnect.
  4. Validate BQ25756 charge and reverse sequences, input loss/return and power budgets on hardware.
  5. Prototype LM51772 only as a candidate for the separate 24 V/10 A output; prove thermal performance and hardware current limit.
  6. Run matched efficiency tests for BQ25756 and ISL81601 only if the analog-controller alternative remains under consideration.
  7. Complete medical isolation/leakage review and test every fault state before product use.
